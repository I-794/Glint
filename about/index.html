Our manifesto

Screenshots deserve better.

A raw screenshot is a record. A good one is a story. Glint exists to make the second kind take seconds, in your browser, without handing your work to anyone.

What we believe

Four ideas shape every feature we ship.

Beautiful by default

You should not need a design degree to make a clean shot. Sensible padding, framing, and color come ready the moment you drop an image in.

Yours, on your machine

The editor renders in your browser. Your images never leave your device unless you choose a cloud feature, and even then you stay in control.

Free, and open

The whole studio is free and the source is public. You can read how it works, fork it, and trust it because nothing is hidden behind a wall.

One canvas, every output

Stills, social sizes, store sets, GIFs, and video all come from the same scene. Compose once and ship it everywhere it needs to go.

How it started

Glint began as a single HTML file. One canvas, a handful of sliders, and a stubborn belief that beautifying a screenshot should not require an account, an upload, or a subscription.

It grew the way good tools do, one real need at a time. Device frames so a screen looked like a screen. Backgrounds so it had somewhere to live. Annotations, mockups, motion, and a color engine, then a layer of AI that drafts the whole look for you. Underneath all of it, the same rule held: everything renders locally, from one source of truth, into one canvas.

That architecture is why the tool stays fast and private. The pixels you export are the pixels your browser drew, on your hardware, from your file. No round trip, no watermark you did not ask for, no surprise.

Built in the open

Read the code, file an issue, or follow what ships next. The project is free and open source, and it stays that way.
